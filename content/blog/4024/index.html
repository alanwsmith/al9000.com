I don't really keep top ten style lists in my head. I forget a ton of stuff as a result. Makes answering this kind of question hard. I love a bunch of fiction. The Peripheral and Dungeon Crawler Carl coming to mind. But, as much as a love a good story, the books I'd send up are non-fiction. Specifically: - Understanding Comics: The Invisible Art by Scott McCloud Don't let the fact that it's a comic book fool you. It's an amazing look at story telling. - A Guide to the Good Life: The Ancient Art of Stoic Joy by William B. Irvine Probably the book that had the most impact on the way I think. Gets into [the hedonic treadmill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedonic_treadmill) and how to get off it. Resetting your expectations of life so you apprecaite it more in the moment. - [A book on personal finance goes here] For the third one, I'm not sure what to pick. I've read a bunch in the category: A Random Walk Down Wall Street, The Bogleheads' Guide to Investing, The Motley Fool Investment Guide, Rich Dad Poor Dad, Think and Grow Rich, The Millionaire Next Door, The Total Money Makeover, The Richest Man in Babylon, etc... It's been a while since I've read any of them. They all kinda blend together. That's not a knock, though. It stems from the fact that the foundational advice is the same across all of them1. - First, debt is toxic. As long as you're under it, you're working for someone else. Your choices in life are limited dramatically as a result. - Second, invest your money in a stock market index fund. The money will grow over the years and decades. Eventually, it'll get to the point where it grows enough each year that you can live off what it makes you instead of having to trade your time for money in a job. Personal finance is one of the most important topics out there. The fact that we fail to teach folks the fundamentals is a fucking crime. -a
### Endnotes - The personal finanace books I listed all have different tones that make more or less sense depending on where you are in life. I've started reading The Simple Path to Wealth, but it's kinda harsh at the start. Different strokes for differnet folks, but maybe not the best pick if you're already feeling the pressure of debt. Maybe start with The Millionaire Next Door (if memory serves it's less judgy feeling). ### Footnotes 1. The Richest Man in Babylon is a bunch of fables. Even though it doesn't get into the specifics of investing in a stock market fund, the central idea is the same. == endfilter == endblock
